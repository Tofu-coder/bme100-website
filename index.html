THE CHALLENGE

Automation
shouldn't be
inaccessible.

Laboratory automation already exists. The question is who can realistically access it.

Large commercial liquid-handling systems can automate repetitive pipetting workflows. This project asks how a similar task might be approached with cost accessibility for academic laboratories in mind.

STATISTICAL CHALLENGES

Automation has a
cost problem.

Laboratory automation has a cost problem before it ever becomes an engineering problem.

Academic laboratories often rely on competitive research grants to fund equipment, personnel, supplies, and other research expenses. When funding is constrained, expensive automation can become difficult to acquire.

FUNDING

Equipment competes with everything else a laboratory needs.

The availability of research funding affects what laboratories can realistically purchase and maintain. A high-cost automation platform may therefore remain inaccessible even when the underlying technology is valuable.

COMPONENTS

Automation also depends on electronic infrastructure.

Liquid-handling systems rely on electronic control systems, computing hardware, and software. These components exist within the broader semiconductor supply chain, connecting equipment accessibility to changes in component availability, demand, and pricing.

CURRENT SOLUTIONS

Automation already works.
The question is access.

LOW-COST / OPEN-SOURCE APPROACH

Opentrons OT-1

Previous work explored modification of a low-cost, open-source commercial liquid-handling platform. The Opentrons OT-1 was discontinued in 2020, but its reported cost of less than $6,000 demonstrated the potential for a much lower-cost approach to automated liquid handling.

LIMITATIONS

The barrier isn't
always the technology.

01

High acquisition cost

Commercial liquid-handling systems can require more than $35,000 in initial investment, limiting the quantity and quality of machines that smaller laboratories can realistically purchase.

02

Maintenance complexity

Intricate specialized components can require calibration, maintenance, and replacement parts. Repair and sustaining costs can make long-term ownership more difficult for smaller laboratories.

03

Difficult interfaces

Existing systems may rely on preprogrammed instructions and specialized software, creating additional training and setup requirements for laboratory users.

DESIGN CRITERIA

Three requirements define a more accessible system.

01

Affordable Unit Cost

Substantially lower the initial capital investment required by academic and small research laboratories.

02

User-Friendly Interface

Provide simple software that laboratory workers can learn quickly, replacing complex rigid scripting and tedious manual setup.

03

Standard Lab Compatibility

Accommodate standard 96-well plates and universal pipette tips without requiring costly proprietary consumables or single-vendor lock-in.
